A CHILD-SAFETY PROJECT

🛡️ Project Guardian

Recognize online grooming warning signs. Get help. Protect children.

Important: This is an early, private prototype—not a professional monitoring service and not proof that someone is a predator. It does not contact police or monitor other apps automatically.

🔍 Check a Message

Paste only the text you are comfortable checking. Remove names, phone numbers, addresses, school names, and other identifying details first.

Your text is checked on this device using simple phrase patterns. It is not sent to a server.

This is a basic phrase-matching demonstration, not an AI model. It can miss danger or flag harmless messages. If something feels wrong, tell a trusted adult even if the result is low concern.

🚨 Get Help Now

If someone is trying to take a child, a child is missing, or danger is happening now, call 911 in the United States. Move to a safer place with a trusted adult if possible. Do not go to meet the person or confront them.

  • Tell a trusted adult immediately. A child should not have to handle this alone.
  • Do not share your location, address, school, passwords, or private images.
  • Save relevant messages and account details if it is safe to do so. Do not forward or download sexual images of minors.
  • Block/report the account after preserving appropriate evidence, unless a trusted investigator advises otherwise.

👨‍👧 Parent Safety Center

  • Set accounts to private and restrict messages, friend requests, and voice chat from strangers.
  • Turn off public location sharing; avoid posting a child's school, routine, or live location.
  • Agree that secret meetups, rides, gifts, or requests to move to private apps must be discussed with a trusted adult.
  • Tell children they can come to you without being punished for telling the truth about a scary conversation.
  • Review safety settings together, in a way suitable for the child's age.

📚 Warning Signs to Know

  • Someone insists a friendship or conversation must be secret from parents or trusted adults.
  • They pressure a child to move quickly to private messaging, share personal information, or send images.
  • They ask for a home address, school, live location, or a secret in-person meeting.
  • They use threats, guilt, flattery, gifts, or blackmail to control the child.
  • They try to isolate the child from friends, family, or other trusted adults.

One clue alone doesn't prove criminal intent. Patterns, context, and a child's safety matter. Children should tell a trusted adult about concerning behavior even when unsure.

📋 Report a Concern

For suspected online child sexual exploitation in the United States, use the official NCMEC CyberTipline:

https://report.cybertip.org/

For immediate danger or a missing child, contact local law enforcement or call 911. Report the account through the platform where it occurred as well.

Keep a factual record of usernames, dates, URLs, and message text where safe. Do not publicly post accusations or try to investigate or confront the person yourself.

🔒 Privacy & Limits

  • This prototype runs its simple text check locally in this page. It does not upload the text or save it after you close or refresh the page.
  • It does not read messages from social media or games, track anyone, identify a real person, or automatically contact authorities.
  • It is not a substitute for a parent, trained child-safety professional, or emergency service.
  • Before any public launch, the project needs security and privacy review, accessible child-friendly design, and careful testing with fictional examples.
